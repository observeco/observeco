§Executive Summary — the core thesis

PetDirectory has built the supply — 534 businesses, 18 categories, #1 on Google for the head term. But demand — pet owners visiting — is essentially zero. A directory with listings but no visitors is a museum, not a marketplace. 534 listings × 0 visitors = 0 value delivered.

The wedge

Nobody owns "the trusted full-service pet directory." Pawwhere is cafe-first, UrbanDogOwner is a guide, Pawshake/PetBacker are booking. The full-service + published pricing + AVS-verified position is open.

The problem

No demand engine. No social, dead forum, 5 blog posts, no reviews, no newsletter. Plus a broken database (20 closed, 76% stale) and a pricing model that charges for value that doesn't exist yet.

The path

Fix leaks → go freemium → build demand → write the book. Content is the compounding moat; the book is the credibility anchor; freemium removes the churn trigger.

The sharp finding — Aylabella Dog Boarding

Its own listing claims "AVS licensed cage-free dog boarding and daycare" — but it is NOT on the March 2026 registry. Either the claim is false, it's licensed under a different legal name, or it got licensed after March. Either way, you are hosting an unverified government-compliance claim.

The liability

If a pet owner books a boarding stay at a facility you listed that turns out to be unlicensed, that's a real harm — and you sent them there. The "trusted" claim depends on trust.

The opportunity

AVS registries are public, downloadable, and no competitor uses them. Badge every boarding/vet listing with "AVS-licensed ✓" — verified against the official registry. That makes "trusted" real instead of a slogan.

6Monetization — the current model is backwards for this stage

Charging S$19-29/month for listings assumes you deliver traffic. With ~0 visitors, a paid listing delivers ~0 value. The subscription model is a stage-3 model applied at stage 0.

Problem 1 — churn

A business that pays S$19/month and gets zero visitors has a concrete reason to leave. The paywall guarantees the churn you're trying to avoid.

Problem 2 — blocks supply

The ~490 licensed businesses you're missing (93 boarding, 291 vets, 102 shops) won't pay to be in an empty directory. Free listings get them in — and supply is your moat.

Problem 3 — mispriced

S$19/mo is too expensive for zero traffic and too cheap for real traffic (a boarding lead is worth S$50-200). The price says "we don't know what to charge."

The right sequence — free to list → build demand → monetize traffic

StageModelWho does this
Now (zero traffic)Free basic listings — remove the barrier, get to 1,000+Pawwhere (free for pet owners)
Traffic arrives (6-18 mo)Freemium — free basic, S$29/mo premium (featured, verified badge, spotlight)Pawwhere's S$89 product
Traffic scales (18+ mo)Lead-gen + ads — S$5-15 per qualified lead, CPC ads, affiliateAngi, Yelp, UrbanDogOwner
The counterargument: "Free listings = no revenue." But the subscription is generating ~0 anyway. The choice is: keep S$19/mo → ~S$1,200/yr + churn risk, or go free → 1,000+ listings → build demand → monetize traffic → a much higher ceiling. The paywall's real cost is the missing supply.

What's actually right about your model

The add-ons are good monetization: pet obituaries (S$50), pet showcase, influencer program (S$9/month). These are emotional, high-value, and differentiated — nobody else has them. The problem is the core listing fee, not the add-ons. And the obituaries are underpriced — pet funeral is a growing, high-margin category; S$50 is cheap for the emotional value.

HIGH is "build a different business" (ads + lead-gen + premium tiers you don't have yet). The ceiling is demand, not supply.
