1. Executive Summary — The Short Version

The good news: You have built something real. 534 businesses listed, 18 categories, the #1 spot on Google for “pet directory singapore”, transparent pricing, and features no competitor has (pet obituaries, pet showcase, influencer program). That is a solid foundation.

The problem: Nobody is visiting. You have worked hard on the supply side — getting businesses listed — but almost nothing has been done on the demand side — getting pet owners to come to the site. A directory with listings but no visitors is a museum, not a marketplace. 534 listings × 0 visitors = 0 value delivered. This is the single biggest issue, and it is also the biggest opportunity.

The second problem: Your pricing model is backwards for where you are today. Charging businesses S$19-29/month for a listing assumes you can deliver them traffic. Right now you can’t. The paywall is keeping businesses out (especially the ~490 licensed businesses you’re missing) and creating a reason for listed businesses to leave when they see no results.

The third problem: The database has quality issues that will hurt you the moment traffic arrives. 20 closed businesses are still listed, 76% of listings haven’t been touched in 90 days, and one business claims to be “AVS licensed” when it is not on the government registry.

What to do (in order): 1. Fix the leaks — remove closed listings, add a “verify this listing” system, check the false AVS claim 2. Go freemium — free basic listings, paid premium tier. Get to 1,000+ listings 3. Build the demand engine — content, videos, social, reviews. This is where the growth is 4. Write the book — establish yourself as the authority on the Singapore pet business landscape

The honest bottom line: PetDirectory can win. The market is real (SGD 412M and growing), the position is open (nobody owns “the trusted full-service pet directory”), and you have the database. But it will not grow by adding more listings. It grows by getting visitors — and that is a content and marketing job, not a listings job.

2.3 The AVS licensing issue (important)

AVS (Animal & Veterinary Service) is the government agency that licenses pet businesses. Commercial pet boarding is only legal at an AVS-licensed facility. AVS publishes public lists of every licensed business — we downloaded them (101 boarding facilities, 320 pet shops, March 2026).

What we found when we cross-checked your listings against the official registry:

  • Boarding: only 4 of your 20 boarding listings are AVS-licensed (Tiny Paw, The Pup Life, VegasPalace, Doggie Retreat)
  • Pet retail: only 15 of 52 match (mostly Pet Lovers Centre branches sharing one license)
  • The sharp one: Aylabella Dog Boarding claims “AVS licensed” in its own listing — but it is NOT on the March 2026 registry. Either the claim is false, it’s licensed under a different name, or it got licensed after March. Either way, you are hosting an unverified government-compliance claim.

The honest caveat: “Not on the registry” does not automatically mean illegal. Some of those 16 are daycare/sitting services that may not need the same license; some may be under different legal names; the registry is a March snapshot. But it flags a real verification gap.

Why this matters to you: 1. It’s a liability. If a pet owner books a boarding stay at a facility you listed that turns out to be unlicensed, that’s a real harm — and you sent them there. 2. It’s your biggest opportunity. The AVS registries are public, downloadable, and no competitor uses them. You could badge every boarding and vet listing with “AVS-licensed ✓” — verified against the official registry. That makes “trusted” real instead of a slogan. 3. Check the Aylabella listing first. It’s the difference between “we verify” and “we host unverified claims.”

3.3 The SEO agency question (you asked — here’s the answer)

Yes, SEO companies are a competitor — and the market is crowded. Your pitch to businesses is “S$19-29/mo for a high-authority backlink that boosts your Google ranking.” That is the same promise SEO agencies sell at S$500-5,000/month. We found 7+ agencies with pet-specific pages (creativeformore, magnified, traffv, petenginemarketing, and others).

The penetration question — how many pet firms already pay for SEO? No public data exists, but the picture is clear: 1. The market is mature. SEO is a well-established S$500-5,000/mo service in Singapore, and the pet vertical is a known agency niche. 2. A pet firm already paying S$800-3,000/mo to an agency will not pay you S$19-29/mo for a backlink — they’ll ask their agency to handle it. 3. The real competitor for S$19-29/mo is FREE. Google Business Profile, Hotfrog, Yelu, Yellow Pages all offer free listings with backlinks. Why pay you? The answer must be traffic — you’re the only directory that actually sends pet-owner visitors. That’s your moat, not the backlink.

The reframe: your backlink pitch is a complement to agency SEO, not a competitor. Agencies buy directory listings as part of their link-building work. The sales pitch becomes: “You’re already paying an agency for SEO. We’re the one directory that sends you actual pet-owner traffic — and we’re cheap.” The backlink is the bonus; the traffic is the product. Agencies become your referral partners, not your rivals.

7. How to Make Money — The Monetization Question

7.1 The current model is backwards for this stage

You charge S$19-29/month for listings. That’s a subscription model — it assumes you deliver traffic. With ~0 visitors, a paid listing delivers ~0 value. Three problems:

  1. It creates the churn it fears. A business that pays S$19/month and gets zero visitors has a concrete reason to leave. The paywall guarantees the outcome you’re trying to avoid.
  2. It blocks supply. The ~490 licensed businesses you’re missing (93 boarding, 291 vets, 102 shops) won’t pay to be in an empty directory. Free listings get them in — and supply is your moat.
  3. It’s mispriced at every stage. S$19/month is too expensive for zero traffic (no value delivered) and too cheap for real traffic (a boarding lead is worth S$50-200). The price says “we don’t know what to charge,” not “we’re confident in our value.”

7.2 The right sequence: free to list → build demand → monetize traffic

Stage Model Who does this
Now (zero traffic) Free basic listings — remove the barrier, get to 1,000+ Pawwhere (free for pet owners)
Traffic arrives (6-18 months) Freemium — free basic, S$29/month premium (featured, verified badge, spotlight) Pawwhere’s S$89 product
Traffic scales (18+ months) Lead-gen + ads — S$5-15 per qualified lead, CPC ads, affiliate Angi, Yelp, UrbanDogOwner

The counterargument: “Free listings = no revenue.” But the subscription is generating ~0 anyway. The choice is: keep S$19/month → ~S$1,200/year + churn risk, or go free → 1,000+ listings → build demand → monetize traffic → a much higher ceiling. The paywall’s real cost is the missing supply — businesses that won’t pay to be in an empty directory.

7.3 What’s actually right about your model

The add-ons are good monetization: pet obituaries (S$50), pet showcase, influencer program (S$9/month). These are emotional, high-value, and differentiated — nobody else has them. The problem is the core listing fee, not the add-ons. And the obituaries are underpriced — pet funeral is a growing, high-margin category; S$50 is cheap for the emotional value.

7.4 The recommendation

  1. Free basic listings — get to 1,000+ (the 93 boarding + 291 vets + 102 shops from the appendix)
  2. Keep the premium tier (S$29/month): featured placement, verified badge, spotlight — businesses self-select once they see traffic
  3. Add lead-gen once traffic arrives — boarding/vet/grooming leads at S$5-15 each
  4. Price the unique features properly — obituaries, showcase, influencer program are your differentiated revenue
  5. The book is itself monetization — sell it, use it as a lead magnet

The honest read: - Low case (~S$16K/year) is achievable with content alone — the “side income” outcome. - Medium case (~S$36K/year) is the realistic target for a solo operator in 18-24 months. - High case (~S$63K/year) requires paid ads, lead-gen, and premium tiers — three things you don’t have yet. Every dollar of effort should go to demand.
