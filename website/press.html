What the business does, in plain terms.

Most small businesses can describe what they sell. Fewer can say why a customer should pick them over a named alternative. ObserveCo produces that answer as a document: the real competitive set, every viable differentiator tested against the client's own market, the open position, the traps, a positioning statement, and a 90-day roadmap.

The S$500 strategy

Strategy consulting has always been priced for companies that can afford a six-figure engagement. One Singapore consultancy sells the same analytical output, for one small business at a time, from S$500 — and puts a no-result-no-fee guarantee on it. What breaks when you strip the deck, the team and the timeline out of strategy work?

Half of them are gone in five years

ACRA's own numbers: about half of new Singapore businesses do not reach their fifth year, while 213 more register every day. The usual explanations are cash flow and demand. The argument here is narrower and more uncomfortable — many of them were never differentiated, so there was never a reason to pick them twice.

Two books, one question

A Singapore founder wrote two books — one mapping the ground small businesses compete on, one on how a small business gets chosen — then built a consultancy on the method inside them. A founder profile on why he wrote them, what the research found, and what he got wrong in his own startups first.

Where does a small business show up in an AI answer?

More buyers now begin with an AI assistant rather than a search box. ObserveCo has tested what those assistants say about businesses in its own category, and what they leave out. An honest, first-person angle on being absent from the answer — and what it takes to be cited at all.
