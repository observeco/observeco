## Hero

- **Sub:** A competitive analysis we produced for GreenPackers, a Singapore compostable-packaging distributor. Real client work, real evidence, real conclusions. This is the depth you get on every engagement.

## Engagement banner
- Why this is here: GreenPackers engaged us for this analysis and consented to its publication as a case study. It's one of several real engagements (Bonefirm, Cai Ca, and a salad-shop business) drawn from work Sean has done since AlgoMerchant and Two Alpha Pte Ltd. We publish real depth and real evidence — and we keep adding cases as outcomes are theirs to share.

## Engagement summary
- The business: Singapore distributor of plant-based compostable packaging — KTR-certified, sourced direct from a Korean manufacturer.
- The question: "Compostable packaging Singapore" is crowded, and the market is greenwash-skeptical. What position can they defensibly own?
- The method: Positioning Module 1 — competitive set, mind map, pricing landscape, adversarial verification.

## What the analysis found
1. **The real problem was BioPak, not the obvious rival.** Verified: BioPak's domain moat (a country subdirectory on a global domain with years of authority), its Deliveroo partnership (80+ products to 4,500 restaurants), and a 440-post education hub. An SEO agency would have told them to outrank it. We told them not to fight it — fight on axes BioPak structurally can't match.
2. **The recommended position: premium-verifiable.** "The only microplastic-tested, home-compostable disposables in Singapore, sourced direct from a real Korean manufacturer." Home-compostable matters because Singapore has almost no industrial-composting infrastructure.
3. **The greenwash flank, verified.** The largest supplier's own eco line is additive-blended PP with no third-party compostability certification — per their own product copy. They admit the microplastic problem in one paragraph and sell the product class that causes it in the next.
4. **The adversarial cert check.** Five certification claims checked against public registries: two didn't resolve. We told them to fix that before competitors' lawyers did. Verification is the product, not a nice-to-have.
5. **The pricing correction.** Their 16oz cup was 1.44× Korean retail for the same manufacturer. The "minimal margins" belief was wrong. Fix: publish carton prices — that's the B2B entry ticket.
6. **The self-correction.** The analysis initially claimed "zero marketplace presence." That was wrong — they have a Shopee store. We corrected it. Rigor means catching your own errors, not just your competitors'.
