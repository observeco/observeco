The Differentiation Playbook · Six white papers

Know the terrain before you pick your position.

Six evidence-backed white papers map the market a Singapore small business actually lives in — the two economies, the four demographic waves, the industry structure, the brands that own it, the force of AI, and the line between understanding your market and finding your position.

The six papers

Each paper tells one idea, with one graphic, using Singapore's own numbers. All figures are confidence-labelled and sourced in the appendix of each paper.

White Paper No. 1 · The Landscape

One island, two economies

The engine — a small club of global firms with most of the value. The domestic layer — almost every firm, serving a market capped at 5.9 million people. And the payroll that flows from one to the other.

White Paper No. 2 · The Demographic Waves

Four waves. Each makes one word scarce.

Ageing, fertility collapse, the shrinking household, and the remaking of care. The business that owns the scarce word — dignity, concentration, convenience, time — rides the tide.

Read it
White Paper No. 3 · The Industry Map

Big numbers are walls. Small is where you compete.

Every industry in Singapore, its size, its trend, and whether the data is good enough to act on. The engine's biggest slices are structurally closed; the small domestic industries are where a position can win.

Read it
White Paper No. 4 · The Brand Map

A wall or a door: which industry are you in?

Some industries a few names own ~80% — Grab, the three banks, FairPrice, PwC. Others no name owns even a fifth — tuition, home care, salons, laundry. The missing brand is the opportunity.

White Paper No. 5 · The AI Map

AI is a two-way wedge.

AI makes the doing of work cheap — execution anyone can copy. It cannot make being chosen cheap — trust, relationship, the word in the customer's mind. That split decides who wins.

Which industries we've mapped — and why

Not every industry qualifies. We only map industries with official, structured data, high fragmentation, and documented competitive pressure. These six are ranked by how likely a differentiation play is to succeed.

Rank Industry Data Why it qualifies
1
 F&B / foodservice Singapore Food Agency (SFA) licensed establishment registry (20K+ establishments, 5,700+ hawker stalls); Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) food-service category SSIC 56111 (1,223 restaurant registrations 2025, +4%); food delivery S$1.6B→S$1.9B Official structured data; very high fragmentation; competition pain verified by insurer QBE's 2025 survey of 600 Singapore SMEs; both buyer types present.
2
 Childcare / preschool Early Childhood Development Agency (ECDA) full centre listing on data.gov.sg (1,900+ centres); Partner Operator (POP) scheme 380 centres / 33 operators; 200K+ places Official registry; fee caps squeeze independents; differentiation pressure.
3
 Tuition / enrichment S$1.4B industry, 1,000+ centres; household spend S$1.8B (2024); marketing arms race documented by the National Institute of Education (NIE) Fragmented, cash-rich, differentiation-desperate.
4
 Beauty / aesthetics Ministry of Health (MOH)-licensed facilities; market ~S$958M by 2032, 13% compound annual growth rate (CAGR); strict advertising rules Saturated clinics; differentiation is genuinely the lever.
5
 Fitness / boutique studios No official registry; The Fit Guide tracks SG studios SG has the highest boutique studio closure rate globally — "differentiation or die" proof.
6
 Pet grooming / vet care Animal & Veterinary Service (AVS) / National Parks Board (NParks) partial data Viable but data-poorer; later candidate.

Verdict: the highest-probability differentiation success sits in F&B (#1), childcare (#2), tuition (#3), and aesthetics (#4) — official data, high fragmentation, documented competitive pressure, and both buyer types present.
