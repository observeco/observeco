# THE FIVE DIMENSIONS — one frame, plain words

**The problem this document fixes.** I have explained these five times and the definitions
still will not stick, because I explained them as five separate ideas. They are not. They
are **one question — "is this business strong?" — asked against five different yardsticks.**

If you know *who the comparison is against*, you know the dimension. Nothing else is needed.

---

## The one question, five yardsticks

| # | Dimension | Wt | "Is it strong…" **compared with WHAT?** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `mental_advantage` | 25% | **compared with other businesses its own size** |
| 2 | `defensibility` | 25% | **compared with a copycat arriving tomorrow** |
| 3 | `competitive_room` | 20% | **compared with the shape of the market** ← not the business |
| 4 | `market_headroom` | 15% | **compared with whether buyers are going unserved** ← not the business |
| 5 | `demand_reach` | 15% | **compared with its own customers** |

**Two of these are not about the business at all.** `competitive_room` and `market_headroom`
describe the *landscape the business stands in*. That is the single largest source of
confusion in the old explanations: I described five business properties, and two of them
were never business properties. (This is also why `competitive_room` is identical for every
competitor in a category — it is measuring the room, not the occupant.)

---

## The five, one at a time

### 1. `mental_advantage` — "do buyers think of you, for your size?"

**Yardstick: other businesses your size.**

The buyer's head has slots — buying occasions ("I need bak chor mee", "someone to do my
nails"). This asks which slots you occupy, and whether that is **more or less than a
business your size should get.**

It is deliberately size-neutral, so that a giant is not automatically the winner and a
one-person business is not automatically last. A giant holding its slot against erosion
counts as strong. A hawker owning one occasion outright counts as very strong.

- **5** = you are the *first* name for a valuable occasion, and rivals are not close
- **3** = you show up about as much as your size predicts
- **1** = you are not retrieved for anything at all

*Tai Hwa Pork Noodle — one hawker stall, 5. True Fitness — 14 clubs, 1.*

---

### 2. `defensibility` — "how fast can someone take it from you?"

**Yardstick: a well-resourced copycat arriving tomorrow.** (Scale is 1–6, not 1–5.)

Two questions, and only two: **how long to copy it**, and **is anything in the way beyond
the work itself?**

Effort you already spent earns nothing. Level 3 = real development work but fully visible —
a committed rival arrives in months. Level 4+ needs a barrier that is not just effort: a
secret process, a patent, a supply deal rivals cannot obtain.

- **"We use a better supplier"** = not defensible (they can use it too)
- **"We developed our own fermentation"** = defensible if rivals cannot see or buy it
- **"Our customers love us"** = love is not a moat

---

### 3. `competitive_room` — "how much space does the market leave?"

**Yardstick: the shape of the market.** ⚠️ **NOT about this business.** The rubric says so
verbatim: *"Do not consider the business's own positioning or quality."*

- **5** = fragmented, no dominant player, no price floor
- **1** = a few giants, pure price war, no margin left for anyone

Read it as **"is this a good neighbourhood to open in"**, not "how good is this business".
A brilliant business in a brutal market scores low here; a mediocre one in an empty market
scores high. That is correct behaviour, not a bug.

---

### 4. `market_headroom` — "are buyers going without?"

**Yardstick: whether demand is being served.** ⚠️ **NOT about this business.**

- **5** = buyers wait, get rationed, turned away, or pay inflated prices because supply
  cannot keep up
- **1** = anyone who wants this can already get it easily

**It is marked `n/a` in 85% of cases** — dropped whenever rivals can simply add supply, so
unmet demand cannot persist. In a bubble-tea market, if buyers want more tea someone opens
another stall; there is no lasting headroom to measure. It only counts where supply is
genuinely stuck (chip fabrication, airport landing slots, a regulated licence).

---

### 5. `demand_reach` — "can you actually find your buyers?"

**Yardstick: its own customers.** Not "is demand big" (that is #4). This is
**addressability**.

- **5** = a tight, known, paying group you can reach directly and cheaply
- **3** = a describable group with a plausible trigger, but no established route to it
- **1** = the customer is undefined

An age band — "30–45" — is explicitly **not** a segment. It fails because there is no
trigger and no channel.

---

## Why this was confusing (three real causes)

**1. Two of the five are not about the business.** Explained above. I described them as if
they were, which made all five sound like they were the same kind of thing.

**2. The instrument you read is not five dimensions.** Measured across 133 runs:

```
mental_advantage   shown in  99% of cases
competitive_room   shown in  99%
defensibility      shown in  98%
demand_reach       shown in  90%
market_headroom    shown in  15%   <- usually absent
```

**78% of cases show exactly four dimensions.** So a description of "five dimensions" never
matches what you see on the page. That mismatch alone would make anyone doubt their reading.

**3. Three different yardsticks sound like the same words.** `market_headroom` and
`demand_reach` both say "demand" and "buyers". `competitive_room` and `market_headroom` are
both "the market". The words overlap; the yardsticks do not.

---

## The honest limit of this frame

**The frame fixes the *description*. It does not fix the instrument.** Two defects survive
it, both recorded in `FINDING-relative-strength-axis.md`:

- **No dimension compares you with your actual competitors.** All five yardsticks above are
  size, a hypothetical copycat, the market, or your own customers — never "the people you
  actually compete with". McDonald's Singapore (150+ outlets, market leader) scores **47**;
  Jollibee Singapore (26 outlets) scores **57**. The construct that would record that does
  not exist yet.
- **`competitive_room` cannot rank rivals** — 19.2% within-category variance, constant in 11
  of 17 categories. Dropping it leaves the within-category order identical in 9 of 15
  categories.

So: this document explains the instrument **as it stands**. It is not a defence of it.
