## Error 1 — MA is RETRIEVAL, not being known

Evidence: **the most famous player does not top its category.**

```
fast-food      McDonald's (150+ outlets, market leader)  MA=3
               KFC                                        MA=4
               Burger King                                MA=4
               Jollibee                                   MA=4
supermarket    NTUC FairPrice (125 stores, largest)     MA=4
               Sheng Siong                                MA=4
               Giant                                      MA=2
```

If MA measured being known, McDonald's would top fast food. It scores 3. And **True Fitness
— a well-known major brand with 14 clubs — scores 1** while it was alive.

MA asks: *when a buying occasion fires, is this business RETRIEVED?* Awareness is not the
same as being called. A business can be recognised by everyone and be nobody's first
thought — the most common real condition, and the whole point of the measure.

## Error 2 — the anchor is the size of the BUSINESS, not the market

Sean wrote *"for the size of your intended market"*. Evidence: **10 tiny home-based
operators score MA=4–5.**

```
MA=4  Bob the Baker Boy                  [home-baking]
MA=4  A gluten-free specialist baker     [home-baking]
MA=4  Frisky Nails                       [home-nails]
MA=4  Lunalili Nails                     [home-nails]
```

Home-based businesses operate in large markets. **If the anchor were market size they would
all score 1–2.** They score 4 because the anchor is what a business *of their own size*
would be expected to own.

## Error 3 — demand_reach is not a capture rate

Sean read it as *"how much of your buyers have you actually found"* — a percentage
achieved. The rubric's levels contain **no percentage and no count**:

```
L1: No identifiable buyer. The customer is undefined, or defined so broadly no one
    can be targeted.
L5: Strong. A tightly defined, concentrated, paying segment that the business can
    reach directly and cheaply.
```

The levels grade **definability and channel existence** — *can* you reach them, not *have*
you. An age band scores 2 precisely because it names no trigger and no channel. It is a
present-tense addressability measure, not a past-tense accomplishment.

## THE REAL FINDING — his two "errors" are better definitions than mine

This is the part that matters. **Both of Sean's wrong readings are more natural, and both
are more useful for a lead magnet, than what the rubric actually says.**

**On MA:** his construct — *"how well known are you to your expected customer"* — is the
reading any business owner will have. My size-relative design was invented to stop small
businesses scoring 1 and being insulted. But **his phrasing contains the fix already**:
*"for the size of your intended market."* Properly anchored:

> **Among the buyers you can actually serve, how familiar are you?**

A home baker's addressable market is its estate, not the island. So this is size-relative
**because the denominator is the addressable segment, not the total market** — Sean's
construct, made workable. That is a better definition than "relative to what a business of
your size would be expected to own", which requires the reader to hold an undocumented
expectation in their head (see `FINDING-relative-strength-axis.md`, which flags that the
size-expectation anchor is undefined).

**On demand_reach:** his construct — *"how much of your buyers have you found"* — is a
**coverage** measure, and coverage is actionable in a way addressability is not.
*"You have found 30% of the buyers in your catchment"* is a far better report line than
*"your segment is describable."*

**Limit to flag:** coverage needs a denominator (how many buyers exist), which is often
unknowable — foot traffic for a bubble tea chain, the estate's household count for a baker.
It can be graded ordinally (more/less of the available segment) without a hard number, but
it cannot be stated as a true percentage.

## What this means for the method

**The playback is a validation instrument, and it just outperformed my six explanations.**
Sean could not understand the definitions and then reconstructed them from scratch. Where he
reconstructed them differently, he was right about what the product needs — twice.

**Rule for this project:** if the domain expert's natural reading differs from the rubric's
wording, and his reading is implementable, **change the rubric's wording.** A definition
that needs six explanations and still misleads the person who commissioned it will mislead
every customer, in the report they receive.

Neither change has been made. Both need Sean's decision, because both alter what the numbers
mean and would require re-running the corpus.
