## What PS measures (rubric 1.20.0, verbatim in spirit)

**How strong is this business's POSITION compared with the positions its competitors hold?**

**⚠ Do NOT score how well known the business is.** A new, unknown business can hold a STRONG position.
A famous business can hold a WEAK one. **Judge the position, never the fame, never the size.**

**⚠ Do NOT score how hard the position is to COPY** — that is defensibility, graded separately.

**The test:** what does this business claim? Is that claim already owned by a named rival? If buyers
encountered it, would they have a reason to prefer it for something specific — **a genuine flank?**

| 1 | **Absent** — no position at all; it makes no claim anyone could prefer it for |
| 2 | **Weak** — the claim is generic, or already owned by a named rival, or matched by everyone in the category |
| 3 | **Adequate** — a defensible slot it holds level with the others; no distinct claim proven against a named rival |
| 4 | **Strong** — a distinct claim against a named rival that buyers would prefer it for; a genuine flank |
| 5 | **Dominant** — the reference occupant of a situation; rivals are defined *against* it |

**⚠ Score per SITUATION, not as one share fight across the whole category.** Positioning is about product
categories, not industries — two businesses in the same industry serving different situations are not
head-on rivals. **A specialist taking a distinct flank in a narrow situation is strong THERE even if it
is small overall.**

## Why these 28

**Mixed on purpose, and deliberately including the profile PS was rebuilt to express:**

- **Big brands (1–4, 14, 17, 24)** — where PS must NOT simply track fame. **If your PS scores here look
  like a fame ranking, the instrument is failing at what it was rebuilt for, and so is the sheet.**
- **Small specialists holding a real flank (5–10, 12, 25)** — these should score WELL despite low
  awareness. **A 4 or 5 here is the whole point of the construct.**
- **Interchangeable operators (13, 20, 21, 28)** — trading, plenty of awareness, **weak or unowned
  claims. These should score LOW.**
- **Same-claim pairs (22 vs 23; 15 vs 16; 20 vs 21)** — if both members of a pair get the same score,
  that is evidence the claim is owned by neither, or by both.
- **A new/emerging business (26)** — **the high-position / low-awareness profile you asked the tool to be
  able to describe.** If PS cannot express this, the construct has failed.

**Grade from memory, and contradicting an earlier reading is fine and useful.**
