# Mental Advantage + Competitive Room — fresh blind grading

Grade **both columns, 1–5 each**, for every business. Use ONLY the definitions below.

**These are fresh readings.** It is fine — useful, even — if they disagree with what you said before.

**Do not look anything up.** Answer from what you already know.

---

## Column A — Mental Advantage (1–5)

**How much does the brand hold in the mind of the customer?**
The share of mind it occupies in its segment — *not* how good it is, and *not* whether it is contested.

| 1 | Unknown — the segment could not name it if asked |
| 2 | Recognised only when prompted — not thought of unprompted |
| 3 | Present and familiar — a known option when the category comes up |
| 4 | Strongly held — comes to mind unprompted, among the first names thought of |
| 5 | Deeply held — the defining name for the category or the occasion |

**⚠ Two traps to watch, both deliberate:**

1. **Mental presence survives closure.** A business that has stopped trading can still hold the mental ladder. Do not lower the score because it closed.
2. **Candour is not weakness.** If a business names its own weakness, that is context, not evidence about what the market thinks.

---



## Column B — Competitive Room (1–5)

**How much space does competition leave this business to operate and earn?**
Higher = more room. **Judge the MARKET, not the business** — ignore its own quality or positioning.

| 1 | No room — dominated by concentrated incumbents, competing almost purely on price |
| 2 | Very little — a dominant player or a severe price floor; structurally squeezed |
| 3 | Some — crowded and price-contested, but gaps exist for a small operator |
| 4 | Good — competitive but fragmented; several players coexist without one crushing the rest |
| 5 | Strong — uncontested and fragmented; no dominant player, no price floor |
