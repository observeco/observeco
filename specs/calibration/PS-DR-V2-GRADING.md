## Column A — Position Strength (1–5)

**For each buying situation it actually competes in: does the market reach for this business by name?**

**⚠ The test is RECOGNITION, not size.** A small specialist that owns a recognised position is strong.
**⚠ Nor is the test how many situations it is in, or how big it is against the whole industry.**

| 1 | Absent — buyers do not weigh it at all |
| 2 | Marginal — one interchangeable option among many similar operators; nothing it is recognised for |
| 3 | At parity — holds its situations level with the other occupants; no clear advantage or disadvantage |
| 4 | Strong — recognised in at least one situation; buyers select it for something specific |
| 5 | Dominant — the reference occupant of at least one situation; others are defined against it |

*(Anchor: Sephora is a 4 here — a recognised position on roughly 7% share. Smallness is not the test.)*

---



## Column B — Demand Reach (1–5)

**Is there an identifiable group of buyers who would pay, AND does this business actually reach them?**

| 1 | No identifiable buyer — the customer is undefined, OR it cannot legally serve the buyers it names |
| 2 | Weak — buyer named only as a demographic with no evidence of current reach; **OR the business has ceased trading** |
| 3 | Moderate — a describable segment it demonstrably reaches, but through a generic or incidental route |
| 4 | Good — a clearly identified segment, reached through at least one credible deliberate channel |
| 5 | Strong — a tightly defined, concentrated, paying segment reached directly and cheaply |

**⚠ Two rules that are easy to get wrong:**

1. **Any business currently trading is at least 3** — being live is evidence it reaches paying buyers. A thin submission is an input-quality problem, not evidence no buyer exists.
2. **A business that has CEASED trading does not get that floor** — it has an identifiable buyer but no longer reaches them, so it is a **2, not a 1**. Level 1 is for *no identifiable buyer* or *cannot legally serve*.
