# DIAGNOSTIC — the C3/C5 ordering: RESOLVED, and my flag was WRONG

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Status:** EXECUTED. The ordering "failure" I reported is **not a failure**. The dimension
was behaving correctly and I misread it.

---

## 1. What I flagged

> "C3 Pet Lovers Centre (largest SEA pet retail, 69 stores, since 1973) scores MA 3/5 — below
> a single hawker stall (5/5) and below a subsidised public gym (5/5). Two candidate causes:
> (a) low-salience category bias, or (b) the relative framing structurally caps large
> incumbents."

I treated "largest scores below smallest" as evidence of a defect.

---

## 2. The decisive test — input sensitivity

**Reading C3's own differentiator answered the second hypothesis immediately.** Every claim in
it is a **size fact**:

> "the **largest** and oldest" · "**widest** and freshest range" · "**footprint no other pet
> retailer here approaches**" · "**our scale** lets us hold national distribution"

Under a *relative* framing, a business whose every claim is a size claim has **nothing to
over-index on** — "at expectation" is the arithmetically correct answer. So either:
- **(a)** the dimension is input-sensitive: score depends on whether the owner *states* a cue; or
- **(b)** the 3/5 is a real finding that PLC owns no retrieval cue beyond scale.

**Test:** identical business, category, price, customer, and derived set. **Only the
differentiator text changes** — adding a situational cue the owner could plausibly have stated
(*"the situation we own is the new pet… when someone brings home their first puppy"*).

**Result:** `D2-petlovers-cue` → **MA 3/5** — **unchanged**, interval 2–4.

**Hypothesis (a) is FALSIFIED.** Adding a stated retrieval cue did **not** move the score. The
dimension is **not** an input artefact; it is reading the competitive structure.

---

## 3. The blank control — and what it revealed

**`D1-watsons`**: dominant incumbent (18% share, 100+ outlets, Euromonitor 2025), low-salience
category, **no distinctive claim at all** — customers pick whichever chain is nearest.

**Result: MA 2/5** (interval 1–2, coverage 0.55), composite **45 Contested**.

---

## 4. The resolution — the ordering is COHERENT

Putting the new cases into the ladder, the top end is not noise. It is a clean ordering by
**distinctive retrieval cue**, and it explains every case including the ones I flagged:

| MA | Case | What it owns |
|---:|---|---|
| **5** | C2 ActiveSG | *"the cheapest way to exercise"* — a distinctive, high-frequency cue |
| **5** | C5 Michelin hawker | *"the best bak chor mee"* — a distinctive cue |
| **4** | KOI | *"trusted everyday tea"* |
| **4** | C6 Eu Yan Sang | 146 years of heritage |
| **4** | C4 Sheng Siong | freshness/value |
| **3** | **C3 Pet Lovers Centre** | **size only — no retrieval cue** |
| **2** | **D1 Watsons** | **none — chosen by proximity** |
| **2** | N2 KOI stripped | differentiator removed |
| **2** | CaiCa | failing |
| **1** | N1 closed | dead |

**C3 at 3 and Watsons at 2 are correct.** The hawker stall beats the pet chain because a
hawker stall is genuinely **retrieved** — it is the first answer to "where do I get great
bak chor mee" — while a 69-store pet chain is **not the first answer to anything**. Customers
walk into whichever branch is nearest.

**This is precisely the Ehrenberg-Bass finding, and my flag inverted it:** *brands are chosen
by being retrieved, not by being large.* Size buys **reach** (distribution, availability) — it
does not buy **retrieval**.

**And the rubric separates the two correctly:** PLC scores `demand_reach` **4** (wide physical
reach) but `mental_advantage` **3**. The hawker is the reverse. **Two different constructs,
measured by two different dimensions.** That is a working rubric, not a broken one.

---

## 5. What I got wrong, stated plainly

I flagged a defect on the reasoning *"the biggest operator should not score below the smallest."*
That reasoning is **wrong under positioning theory** — it assumes size and mental availability
move together, which is the exact assumption the CEP literature exists to refute.

**This is the second time in this project that a flagged defect turned out to be the instrument
working.** The first was the "low confidence" theme (it was evidence coverage). Both times the
cause was the same: **I judged the output against an intuition instead of against the theory the
dimension implements.**

---

## 6. What remains genuinely open

The diagnostic resolved the *ordering*. It does not resolve the **top anchor**, which is a real
and separate concern:

**C5 (one hawker stall) and C2 (a national programme) both score 5/5, and C5's interval came
back 5–5 at 0.80 coverage** — a decisive maximum. These are different *kinds* of advantage:
- C5 **punches enormously above its weight for its size**, but is not the default answer for a
  mass-market buyer;
- C2 **is the default answer for cheap exercise nationally**.

The relative framing merges "over-indexes for its size" with "is the market's first retrieval."
That merge is what lets one stall tie a national programme. **Sean's call to split the top
anchor is correct and is the next step.**
