# FINDING — Price premium as a label: TESTED. Usable, but only in some categories, and it breaks the proposed screen

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Trigger:** Sean — *"I'm treating your proposals as hypothesis that need testing to ground the
effectiveness. Try it out and tell me whether it works?"*
**Script:** `test_price_premium_label.py`
**Verdict:** **H1 (measurable) PASSES. H2 (discriminates) PASSES, with a limit that was not
predicted. H3 (financial outcome does not proxy positioning) PASSES — and it also falsifies Sean's
proposed revenue-direction screen, which would have shown Ezra as a growth company through the
entire period its future was being destroyed.**

---

## 1. H1 + H2 — PASSES, but the label is CATEGORY-CONDITIONAL

**The test:** measure price spread across two categories where positioning theory makes opposite
predictions.

| category | lowest | highest | **spread** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Bubble tea, SG** (rep. milk tea) | S$1.50 (Mixue) | S$5.50 (Chicha San Chen) | **267%** |
| **Petrol RON95, SG** (22 Sep 2026) | $3.48 | $3.49 | **0.3%** |
| Petrol diesel, SG | $3.97 | $4.07 | 2.5% |

**Ratio of spreads: 928×.**

**This is the result that matters, and it is not the one I predicted.** I proposed price premium as a
general label. It is not general — **it is defined only where price spreads.**

- **Bubble tea: positioning is active.** Brands occupy genuinely distinct price positions across a
  267% range. A premium is *readable* — you can tell who is charging more and, more importantly, who
  is *sustaining* it.
- **Petrol RON95: price is arbitraged to convergence.** All five major chains within **one cent**.
  A 1-cent gap cannot carry a positioning signal — there is nothing there to measure.

**So the honest version of the label is conditional:**

> **Price premium is a valid positioning label in a category where price spreads. In a category
> where price has converged, the ABSENCE of spread is itself the finding** — it tells you positioning
> is not operative and competition has commoditised to cost. **The label has two outcomes, not one.**

This is the same shape as A3: the measure exists only where the phenomenon can express itself.
**And it needs the same treatment — a condition check before the measure.**

---

## 2. The in-category outcome experiment — directional but thin

Bubble tea gives us deaths and survivors **in the same category, same period**:

| | brand | detail |
|---|---|---|
| **DEAD** | **Gong Cha** | shut **2 Oct 2025**, all 29 SG outlets, no replacement franchisee found |
| **DEAD** | **Tiger Sugar** | domain expired 1 Aug 2026 |
| ALIVE | 5 of 11 sit in a narrow S$3.60–5.50 band | |
| ALIVE | Mixue at the floor (S$1.50) | entered 2022, "rewrote the value end" |
| ALIVE | Chicha San Chen, HEYTEA at the top (S$5.50) | premium pricing, both surviving |

**The read:** Gong Cha died **mid-tier with no distinct position** — the position our rubric would
predict is weakest. Mixue won by **creating a new price floor**, not by positioning up — which is
category creation, not a premium. Premium brands survive alongside it.

**Directionally consistent with positioning mattering. Not established.** n=2 deaths, and I cannot
attribute cause from price alone — Gong Cha's exit could be franchisor-level, not positioning.
**Recorded as directional evidence, not validation.**

## 3. H3 — PASSES, and it kills the proposed screen

**This is the most valuable result of the test.**

### Ezion — revenue down 9%, profit DOUBLED

```
FY2014   revenue 386.5m    net profit 40.5m
FY2015   revenue 351.1m    net profit 83.7m   <- revenue -9.2%, profit +107%
```

**A revenue-direction screen mis-signals in-year.** Revenue down, business improving.

### Ezra — grew revenue for a decade, then died

Listed 2003. Bought 30+ vessels, 2 FPSOs, the Aker subsea division (2011). **Revenue rose
continuously on debt-funded expansion.** FY2016 revenue "weaker than FY2015" — and net loss after
tax **> US$1 billion**; by 2017 it was suspending payments on ~US$2 billion of debt.

**Revenue decline was a LATE signal.** By the time it fell, the outcome was set years earlier.

**Therefore the screen Sean proposed — sort by revenue direction, investigate the decliners — would
have classified Ezra as a GROWTH company through the entire period in which its future was being
destroyed.** It would have placed the single best example of a positioning failure into the healthy
bucket.

**And the signal that *would* have caught it — leverage taken on during the growth phase — is a
FINANCIAL signal, not a positioning one.** Which is precisely why financial outcome cannot be the
label.

**H3 is confirmed by the mechanism, not just asserted:** the screen fails because revenue and
positioning are different variables, and Ezra is the case where they diverged maximally.

---

## 4. What this means for the sample design

**Adopt:**
- **Price premium to category as the primary label — CONDITIONAL on the category spreading price.**
  Gate it exactly as A3 gates headroom: check first, measure only where measurable.
- **Category-converged categories yield a finding, not a null.** "Positioning is not operative here"
  is a real result and is reportable.
- **Financial outcome demoted to a confirming signal** where positioning-driven failure is
  independently established (Ezra: yes, it died of undifferentiation. Hyflux: no, it died of
  leverage).

**Drop:**
- **The revenue-direction screen.** Falsified by Ezra and Ezion. It detects distress late and
  mis-signals in-year.

**Sample implications — the label needs categories that spread price.** Bubble tea qualifies (267%).
Petrol does not (0.3%). **So case selection must first verify the category is one where price
disperses**, or the label is undefined for that case.

---

## 5. What this does NOT establish

- **The premium label is not yet validated as a RUBRIC predictor.** I have shown it is *measurable*
  and *discriminating in principle*. I have **not** shown that the rubric's `mental_advantage` or
  `defensibility` scores track it. **That is the actual test and it has not been run.**
- **n=2 deaths in the natural experiment.** Directional only; cause not attributable from price.
- **Price premium has confounds I have not separated:** premium can come from brand spend, a locked-in
  installed base, switching costs or distribution — **not only positioning.** A premium is consistent
  with strong positioning; it is not proof of it.
- **Prices are list prices, not net of promotions.** The sources show heavy app/loyalty discounting
  (KOI 10%, CHAGEE 15% Wednesdays, PlayMade 10%). **Realised price may be flatter than list** — which
  would compress the measured spread. Unquantified.
- **The two categories are not a sample.** Petrol was chosen as a deliberate contrast; that is a
  designed comparison, not a random draw. **It establishes the mechanism, not a distribution.**
- **Ezion/Ezra numbers are from results announcements recovered by search**, not from audited filings
  read directly. One figure (Ezion FY2014 revenue) came from a market-data summary.
- **Still zero rubric-vs-human-label comparison.** This test moved the label question forward and did
  not touch the validation blocker.
