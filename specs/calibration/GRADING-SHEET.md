## Read this first

**What I want from you is a judgement, not a check of my arithmetic.** Grade
independently — ideally without reading my column first. An echo of my own scores

the extra 92 will not rescue it, and you will have saved your time.

### Four things about the scales that will change your numbers

1. **`mental_advantage` is RELATIVE to business size.** A sole operator is NOT
   compared with a national chain on absolute recall — each is judged against what a
   business *its size* would be expected to own. A home baker owning its estate's
   birthday occasions can score 4.
2. **`defensibility` is TIME-TO-COPY and OBSTRUCTION**, never effort spent. Level 3 =
   real development work but fully visible (a committed rival arrives in months).
   Level 4+ needs a barrier beyond effort: a trade secret, patent, undisclosed process,
   or a supply arrangement rivals cannot obtain.
3. **`market_headroom` is DEMAND vs SUPPLY, not crowdedness.** I mark it **n/a** where
   capacity is elastic (rivals can add supply, so unmet demand cannot persist). Grade
   it only where buyers genuinely cannot get what they want.
4. **`competitive_room` is CHALLENGER-FRAMED.** High = fragmented, room for a small
   operator. A dominant incumbent therefore *lowers* this score. If that reads wrong
   for a market leader, say so — it is one of the five things I most want attacked.
