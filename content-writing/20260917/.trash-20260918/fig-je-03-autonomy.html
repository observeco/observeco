The under-read feature — not speed, not price

The Confidence Number Is the Product

Calibrated probability is what lets a system draw the line between "the machine decides" and "a human decides". That line is the difference between an assistant and an operator.

"If a model can do a task 95% of the time but doesn't say when it's in the 5%, it can't automate that task."

TypeSafe AI, launch post, 15 Sep 2026 — the sentence the coverage keeps under-reading.

Confidence above the line

The system acts

Auto-execute, no human in the loop. The decision was cheap, instant, and honest about its own certainty.

On the line

It asks once

One escalation, exactly when the probability says it needs one. Not on every step forever.

Below the line

A human decides

The hard cases still go to a person. The threshold is an engineering dial, not a leap of faith.

The blocker was never intelligence

Speed and cost make Jev interesting. Calibrated probability is what makes automation possible. Today every AI system needs a human on every step because the model cannot say when it is in the 5%. The moment every decision carries an honest probability, tiered autonomy becomes buildable — and measurable.

Pattern it like a CPU: L1 the fast cheap decider, L2 the slow expensive reasoner. Route everything through L1 first; escalate only what is actually hard.

Cheaper decisions

Do not reduce the number of decisions. They multiply. That is the Jevons paradox the company is named for — every order-of-magnitude drop in the cost of a decision makes previously uneconomical decisions worth making.
