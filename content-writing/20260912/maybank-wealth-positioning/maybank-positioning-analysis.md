# Positioning Analysis of the Maybank Ad — Working Draft

## The core failure: this ad is a non-positioning statement in a sea of identical non-positioning statements

Read it once, cold, as the intended customer would. "A Refined Perspective. Tap into insights from across Asia, with expert perspectives on market trends and opportunities." Now imagine it on a DBS, UOB, OCBC, Citi, HSBC, StanChart ad. Nothing would change. That is the first, fatal clue: **the copy is interchangeable.** Positioning is the act of claiming a spot in the customer's mind that no one else can occupy. If you can swap every logo on the page and it still works, you have not positioned anything — you have decorated a generic promise with your wordmark.

## Positioning theory lens

Positioning, in the Ries & Trout sense that still governs the category, is **not about the product. It's about occupying a distinct place in the prospect's mind against named competition.** Three tests decide whether an ad has a position or is just noise:

### Test 1 — Who is the specific customer with a specific job?
"Refined" + "curated" + "wealth management" + "Speak with us" addresses no one in particular. Is it the 45-year-old Singaporean entrepreneur rolling out of a Series B? The Malaysian family office deciding whether to move money offshore? The Jakarta professional wanting shariah-compliant growth without a US bank? The ad answers none of these. It says "someone with money, probably." That is a demographic, not a person with a job-to-be-done.

### Test 2 — What is the ONE thing you win on, and which competitor does it unseat?
The ad lists four overlapping promises: insights, expert perspectives, a curated selection, world of investment opportunities. Four middling claims beat out one sharp claim. And critically — **nothing names a competitor or the category's status quo.** Positioning works by contrast ("faster than X", "for people who are tired of Y"). This says "we have nice things" with no tension.

### Test 3 — Does the creative and copy agree on one mental category?
"Refined Perspective" is heritage-luxury grammar: muted cream, warm gold, serif elegance, passive promises. But the actual proposition — Maybank Securities, curated investment opportunities — is a functional banking promise. The ad wears an old-money evening gown while trying to sell you a stock portfolio. The category in the customer's head ("who am I making rich, gently") never connects to the category in the bank's head ("we have a securities arm").

## The deeper betrayal: Maybank's real advantage is in its DNA and this ad buries it

Maybank is not a generic global wealth brand. It is the bank of ASEAN — Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia home markets, present in all ten ASEAN nations, founded 1960 by a Singaporean tycoon, with the largest Islamic bank in the region bolted on. Its genuine, defensible, competitor-free position is: **the first and most natural bank for wealth that moves across Southeast Asia, including the Muslim majority that the global-private-bank logos barely speak to.** That is a real edge, held against DBS/UOB/OCBC (Singapore-centric) and Citi/HSBC/StanChart (global but locally thin).

The ad could have said: "We are the wealth bank for the ASEAN corridor." Instead it said "A refined perspective." It took its birthright and spent it on a beige adverb.

## So why doesn't it connect, precisely?

1. **No named customer, no named job.** Generic affluent-portrait.
2. **No contrast, no tension.** Nothing to overcome, nothing it's better at, no competitor unseated.
3. **Self-description, not benefit.** "We have curated selection" ≠ "Your money stops being stuck at one border."
4. **Visual and verbal category mismatch.** Oldwealth visuals, functional promise.
5. **It flatters the bank, not the reader.** "A refined perspective" is the bank admiring its own wallpaper.
6. **CTA has no cost-reversal.** "Speak with us" puts the work on the customer with no reason to raise their hand.

## The correct approach — one positioning sentence

Positioning sentence for a redesign:
> **For the affluent person whose wealth genuinely spans ASEAN — who lives, earns, and invests across Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia — Maybank is the wealth bank that treats the whole region as one home market, not a set of foreign markets; the only one that pairs ASEAN-native reach with the region's largest Islamic wealth capability.**

Wedge for the ad: **"The one bank where ASEAN is home, not foreign."** Or sharper, the humanizing Maybank brand promise: **"We see your money the way you live it — across borders, not confined to one."**

Every element of the redesign (which Spectrum builds) must trace to that single sentence.
