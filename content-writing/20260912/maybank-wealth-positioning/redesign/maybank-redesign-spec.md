# Maybank Wealth Management — Positioning Redesign
**Product Designer: Spectrum** · ObserveCo Consulting light-first design system · 1200 × 672 landscape ad / landing hero

> Files in this folder: `maybank-redesign-ad.png` (the ad), `cover-mockup.png` (framed cover presenting the work), plus their HTML sources and render scripts.

---

## 1. Design brief

The original Maybank Wealth Management ad fails all three positioning tests at once. It addresses no specific customer ("someone with money, probably"), it piles four overlapping promises (insights, expert perspectives, curated selection, world of opportunities) so it wins none, and it dresses a *functional* securities promise in *heritage-luxury* visuals (cream, gold, charcoal — "a refined perspective"). Worst of all, "A REFINED PERSPECTIVE" actively contradicts Maybank's own stated mission — **"Humanising Financial Services"** — by reading cold, exclusive and self-congratulatory, and its CTA ("Speak With Us") dumps all the work on the reader with no reason to raise their hand.

The redesign throws away the heritage costume and positions Maybank on the one thing no competitor owns: it is the **bank of ASEAN — home markets in Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia, present in all ten ASEAN nations, with the region's largest Islamic bank built in**. The customer is now a real person with a real job-to-be-done; the promise is a single, concrete cross-border benefit; and the CTA reverses the cost of acting.

---

## 2. The wedge (hero line)

> **"You don't cross borders. Why should your wealth?"**

This was chosen deliberately from the three options. It is the most *human* and the most *reversible*: it names the customer's actual lived experience (they cross borders), turns the abstract "wealth" into a relatable subject, and opens a gap ("your money shouldn't feel it belongs anywhere else"). It lands the whole positioning sentence in one breath, and it is unmistakably Maybank's — no Singapore-centric or global bank could honestly use it, because none of them treats ASEAN as one *home* market.

The kicker on the ad reads **"ASEAN is home, not foreign"** — the second wedge option — used as a supporting overline that reinforces the headline without competing with it.

## 3. Full copy deck

**Headline**
> You don't cross borders.
> Why should your wealth?

**Supporting line (over the headline, as kicker)**
> ASEAN IS HOME, NOT FOREIGN

**Subhead (the one benefit, in the customer's words)**
> You live, work and invest across Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia. One trusted bank treats the whole region as a single home market — so your money moves the way you do, without ever hitting a foreign wall.

**Supporting benefit line (the second, complementary proof — the Islamic edge)**
> ✓ Shariah and conventional wealth under one roof — backed by the region's largest Islamic bank.

**Cost-reversing CTA**
> **Get your free 30-minute cross-border review →**

**CTA reassurance line (the cost-reversal made explicit)**
> **No obligation** · No fees for the first call. Your plan, either way.

**Brand lock-up**
> **MAYBANK** / *Wealth Management*
> watermark: *Humanising Financial Services*

*Why each element:* the headline is the wedge; the subhead turns it into a concrete, benefit-led promise ("one trusted partner, whole region, no foreign wall"); the supporting line adds the single most defensible proof in the verified facts (largest Islamic bank in ASEAN) without fabricating product names or numbers; the CTA reverses the cost of action ("free," "no obligation," "no fees" for the first call) instead of the cold "Speak With Us."

## 4. Visual direction

**Palette** — ObserveCo consulting light-first paper, warm and human, with Maybank's yellow-green and the consulting teal as accent. *No old-brown luxury.*
- Canvas `#f7f6f3` (warm bond paper)
- Ink `#1b1f24` / secondary `#4a525c` / tertiary `#6b737e` (near-black, not pure black — friendly)
- Insight/teal `#0e6e5c`, strong `#0a5447`, tint `#e7f1ee` (the primary accent + CTA)
- Warm `#c9892e` (the heritage nod, used *sparingly* for the route line + kicker rule)
- Maybank yellow-green `#7db13a` in the brandmark gradient
- Warm paper panel `#f3ead7 → #e5d9bf` for the photography ground — soft cream-gold that reads warm and grounded, not "old money"

**Type** — Serif display (Iowan/Palatino) for the headline's authority and human warmth, matching the consulting system's "serif display = authority" token; Inter sans for body, labels and CTA (legible, approachable). This is the *consulting* system, not heritage gold-serif.

**Imagery / layout** — Left two-thirds is the copy zone; right third a warm editorial panel (like a Polaroid / region map). Inside the panel: a small human family motif (the "humanising" cue — a parent with a child, warm and ordinary, not a distant banker), and a **dashed route connecting Kuala Lumpur → Singapore → Jakarta** — the actual home-market corridor — drawn in the warm accent. City labels in italic serif keep it editorial. Chips ("One home market," "Shariah + conventional," "10 ASEAN nations") plus the "Humanising Financial Services" watermark quietly reinforce position and brand DNA. The visual and the copy now agree on one mental category: *you and your money moving across one home region*, grounded in real places and real people.

## 5. How the design passes the three positioning tests

**Test 1 — A specific customer with a specific job.**
Instead of "someone with money," the ad names the job in the headline and subhead: the affluent professional/business owner **living, working and investing across Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia** — and the specific job is *"keep my wealth moving freely across my three home markets."* The copy speaks to that exact person; the route map draws their actual geography.

**Test 2 — The ONE thing you win on, and which competitor it unseats.**
The subhead makes a single owned claim: **ASEAN is one home market, not foreign markets** — and the supporting line adds the one defensible proof (largest Islamic bank in ASEAN). It implicitly unseats the status quo — Singapore-centric DBS/OCBC/UOB (they reach ASEAN; they're not *of* it) and globally-thin Citi/HSBC/StanChart (ASEAN is a feature for them, not home). There is tension and contrast now, not "we have nice things."

**Test 3 — Creative and copy agree on one mental category.**
Everything — headline ("you cross borders"), image (family + KL→SG→Jakarta route), color (warm paper + teal + human green) — points to **"you and your regional wealth, treated as home."** No more old-heritage gown over a functional banking promise. And it is unmistakably *human* ("Humanising Financial Services"), not exclusive: it flatters the reader's life, not the bank's wallpaper.
