# Why "A Refined Perspective" Doesn't Connect

## A positioning autopsy of a Maybank Wealth Management advertisement

Before you read on, try this. Cover the "MAYBANK" wordmark at the top of the ad, and read the rest of it back to yourself.

*A Refined Perspective. Tap into insights from across ASIA, with expert perspectives on market trends and opportunities. A Selection Curated For You. Access a world of investment opportunities, curated around your goals. Speak With Us.*

Now put "DBS" in place of Maybank. Then "OCBC." Then "UOB." Then "Citi." Then "HSBC."

Nothing breaks. Nothing even blinks.

That is the whole problem in one paragraph. But it's worth slowing down to understand *why*, because the fix — and the reason most bank advertising fails — is hiding in plain sight.

---

## First, what is positioning, actually?

Positioning is not a word you put on a poster. It's a *place you occupy in someone's mind*. The founding insight — from Ries & Trout, back when they wrote *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* in 1981 — is brutal and still true:

> You do not position the product inside the product. You position the product **inside the prospect's head**, and you do it **against named competition**.

Three tests tell you whether an ad has a position or just has nice words:

1. **Who is the specific customer, and what specific job are they trying to get done?** Not "someone with money." A real person with a real decision in front of them.
2. **What is the ONE thing you win on, and which competitor or status-quo does it unseat?** Positioning works by contrast. If there's no contrast, there's no position.
3. **Do the words and the visuals agree on a single mental category?** The customer should arrive at the same thought from the copy and from the picture.

Any ad that fails all three isn't "bad marketing." It's a non-statement wearing a logo. And this Maybank ad fails all three, at once.

---

## The autopsy

### 1. It addresses nobody, so it means nothing to anybody

"Refined. Curated. A world of investment opportunities." Who is this for?

- The 44-year-old Singaporean who just sold part of her business and needs to decide whether to park proceeds across one border or three?
- The Malaysian family considering whether a shariah-compliant portfolio can outgrow a conventional one?
- The Jakarta professional who keeps his cash split between three banks because none of them feel like home?

The ad was written for none of these people. It was written for a *demographic* — "someone with money, probably" — and you cannot position to a demographic. You position to a person with a decision to make.

### 2. It lists four promises, so it wins nothing

Count them: *insights*, *expert perspectives*, *a curated selection*, *a world of opportunities*. Four middling claims, each one instantly substitutable with any of its rivals.

Positioning isn't a laundry list. It's a commitment to one thing — and that means *leaving things out*. "We are the best at four things" is a sentence no customer has ever believed. "We are the only bank that does *this*" is a sentence they remember.

And crucially: there is no tension anywhere. Nothing says "faster than", "broader than", "more native than", "for people who are tired of". No competitor is unseated. The ad is a photograph of the bank admiring its own wallpaper.

### 3. The words and the picture disagree on what this is

Look at the palette: cream, soft brown, warm gold. That is heritage-luxury grammar — think old private banks, mahogany, the word "legacy." Now look at what's actually being sold: *Maybank Securities*. Curated investment opportunities. A functional stock-and-wealth promise.

The ad is wearing an old-money evening gown while trying to hand you a prospectus. One half of your brain files it under "exclusive heritage brand; I'm not worthy / this is for my grandfather." The other half files it under "bank product; where do I click." Those two files are in different rooms. The ad never picks a room, so it lands in neither.

### 4. And here's the one that stings

Maybank's own mission, on its own website, in its own words, is **"Humanising Financial Services."**

*Humanising.* Warm. Personal. In one of their own wealth headlines: "Why Will/Wasiat Is Not Just for the Wealthy." Inclusive. Real. People-shaped.

And then the ad says "A Refined Perspective."

"Refined" is the opposite of "humanising." It's the brand's stated purpose, inverted. Maybank spent decades building a promise around being the human, accessible, home-bank of ASEAN — and the wealth ad threw it away to sound like every other private bank in the directory.

That's not a copywriting slip. That's the brand's competitive advantage, walked right past, to go stand in the middle of a crowd shouting nothing.

### 5. And the CTA puts all the work on the customer

"Speak With Us." Raise your hand, walk into the room, start the conversation, and by the way we haven't told you one concrete thing you get. That's a CTA with no cost-reversal. No reason for anyone to make the first move, and no reason to prefer Maybank over the seven other banks asking the same thing.

---

## What Maybank actually owns (and the ad pretends it doesn't)

Here's where it gets interesting, because the raw material for a genuinely unassailable position is sitting right there.

Maybank is not a generic international wealth brand. It is **the bank of ASEAN**:

- Home markets in Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia — a network spanning **all ten ASEAN nations**, plus key Asian centres and global financial hubs.
- **Maybank Islamic is the largest Islamic bank in ASEAN** by assets — named Global Islamic Bank of the Year by *The Banker*.
- Founded 1960 by a Singaporean entrepreneur; Malaysia's largest bank and one of the largest in Southeast Asia by assets.
- An integrated ecosystem: Maybank Securities, Maybank Asset Management, Maybank Investment Bank, Maybank Trustees, and Etiqa insurance and takaful under one roof.

Now line that up against the field.

## The whitespace no one owns

| Bank | Segment | Position (verbatim or paraphrase, from their own pages) | The angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| DBS | Treasures / Treasures Private Client / Private Bank | "Asia's global wealth gateway." | Asia connectivity + digital leadership |
| UOB | Wealth / Privilege / Private Bank | "Your trust deserves the best"; a Private Banking experience with a "uniquely ASEAN heritage"; "One Bank approach across ASEAN." | ASEAN heritage, relationship, multigenerational succession |
| OCBC | Premier / Premier Private Client | "ASEAN and Greater China. A land where wealth flourishes." | ASEAN + Greater China corridor |
| Citi | Citigold | "Premium banking lifestyle & travel." | Global lifestyle, preferred pricing |
| HSBC | Premier / Premier Elite | "A path to true wealth"; a lifetime of privileges. | Global mobility + expat lifestyle |
| Standard Chartered | Priority / Private | "bespoke wealth solutions… to grow across borders" | Global open-architecture, markets |
| CIMB | Preferred / Private Wealth / Private Banking | Full-tier suite incl. an Islamic wealth pillar | Malaysian rival with Islamic muscle |
| RHB | Premier | "exclusively designed for you and your family" | Malaysian family wealth |

Now watch what all eight actually compete on. Read down that "angle" column: **gateway, heritage, corridor, lifestyle, privileges, open-architecture.** Every one of them is selling status, access, or perks. Nobody is leading with purpose — with what your money is *for*. Nobody brands itself as the family-wealth-inheritance specialist, even though half of them quietly offer succession planning as a feature. And the regional story is fragmented: DBS is a global gateway, UOB is ASEAN, OCBC is ASEAN-plus-China, RHB is Malaysia. None of them speaks to the full, lived geography of a modern Southeast-Asian family.

The closest any competitor comes to Maybank's ground is **UOB**, which already claims "uniquely ASEAN heritage." So honesty requires saying exactly what Maybank owns that UOB — and everyone else — does not:

- **A physical footprint across all ten ASEAN nations, completed by 2012** (UOB leans Malaysia–Singapore–Thailand–Indonesia; it is not the all-ten bank).
- **The region's largest Islamic bank bolted on** — Maybank Islamic. No regional rival can claim that.
- **A stated purpose to humanise** ("Humanising Financial Services"), not to confer status — the one thing none of the eight is selling.

That is the gap: **DBS, UOB and OCBC are Singapore-centric banks reaching out to the region. Citi, HSBC and StanChart are global banks thin on the ground. Nobody owns actually *being* home — the all-ten-ASEAN bank with the largest Islamic wealth capability and a mandate to humanise, not intimidate.**

That is Maybank's birthright, and the ad spent it on a beige adverb.

---

## The correct approach

A position is one sentence you would defend. Here is Maybank's:

> **For the affluent person whose wealth genuinely spans ASEAN — who lives, earns, and invests across Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia — Maybank is the wealth bank that treats the whole region as one home market, not a set of foreign markets. It's the only one that pairs ASEAN-native reach with the region's largest Islamic wealth capability, wrapped in a promise to humanise, not intimidate.**

Shorter, for the poster: **"The bank where ASEAN is home, not foreign."**

Or lead with the humanising promise: **"We see your money the way you live it — across borders, not confined to one."**

There's a second, quieter wedge in the same data, if Maybank wants to lead with legacy instead of geography: **every competitor sells status and perks; none is the family-inheritance specialist, none leads with what the money is *for*** — despite half of them quietly offering succession planning. Maybank's own content already leans this way ("Why Will/Wasiat Is Not Just for the Wealthy"). A family/legacy-led take could own that word instead of "refined." Either wedge beats a beige adverb; the point is to pick one and let every element of the ad answer to it.

Now every element of a redesigned ad earns its place against that single sentence. The customer becomes specific (the ASEAN-affluent person with cross-border wealth). The one thing we win on becomes sharp (ASEAN is home). The competitor gets contrasted (Singapore banks reaching out; global banks reaching in). The palette stops playing "old money" and starts playing "your home region, warm and human." And the CTA gives the customer a reason to raise their hand.

That's what I'll build next — because talk is cheap, and the point of this exercise was never to critique. It was to prove the theory moves, from critique to a redesign you could actually run.
