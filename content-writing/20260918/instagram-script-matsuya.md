## Why v1 was disconnected (the diagnosis)

v1 had six beats and each was a **separate fact placed in sequence**. Store counts, then halal certification, then a recommendation. Nothing in one beat caused the next beat, so the video felt like a list of observations rather than an argument. A viewer can follow a list, but they don't feel pulled through it.

**v2 fixes this by making every beat answer "so what?" from the beat before it:**

| Beat | Answers |
|---|---|
| #3 is opening in #1's building | → so what? |
| In Japan, #1 beat #3 on price | → so what? |
| So if it fights on price here, it loses the same way | → but what actually decides it here? |
| Halal. Both rivals have it, Matsuya hasn't announced | → so what should it do? |
| Sell the one thing nobody here sells | → close |

One thread. Same argument the article makes, compressed.

**Two words removed per Sean:** "scorecard" (imported metaphor, needs decoding) and "that isn't a fight, that's a test" (abstract, states a feeling rather than a reason). Both replaced with plain, concrete phrasing.

## Minimum credible duration — the honest answer

**This script runs 50 seconds. The credible floor is ~40 seconds. I would not go below that for this account.**

Here is the reasoning, because it's a real trade-off and not a preference.

**What makes it credible rather than a fun fact** is the halal beat — that Singapore's Japanese fast-casual market is decided partly on halal certification, that both rivals already hold it, and that Matsuya's status is unannounced. That is the one non-obvious thing in the whole piece. Without it, the video is "a chain is opening in the same mall as its bigger rival," which is trivia.

So the credible minimum is the three load-bearing parts and nothing else:

| Element | Time | Can it be cut? |
|---|---|---|
| The collision (hook) | ~5s | No — it's the reason to watch |
| The real axis (halal) | ~11s | **No — this is the credibility** |
| The answer (set meal) | ~9s | No — otherwise it's a complaint, not a lesson |
| Setup + price-war bridge | ~17s | Partly — can compress to one line each |
| Close | ~8s | No — it's the point |
| **Total** | **50s** | |

**Under 40 seconds, something has to go, and the only honest thing to cut is the halal beat.** That leaves a 25–30s video that gets the hook and the advice but drops the evidence — exactly the "fun fact" version. It would probably get more views and be worth less.

**Why not longer?** 60s also works and gives more breathing room. But this argument is three steps — collision, real axis, answer — and stretching it invites padding, which is precisely what made v1 feel disconnected. **50s is where it's tight and complete.**

**If Sean wants 30s:** cut the halal beat and the price-war bridge, keep hook → set meal → close. That's a 30s video that is punchy and true but demonstrates no research. My recommendation is to keep 50s, because the credibility *is* the differentiator on this account.

## Accuracy guard (do not drop this)

The hoarding is a **sighting by a passerby**, reported by Mothership — **not an official Matsuya or Suntec City announcement.** Small on-screen line in the first 5 seconds:

> `Hoarding sighting · not yet officially announced`

Two claims to keep soft:
- **Outlet counts conflict** across sources. Both figures used are verified: Sukiya "over two thousand" (Japan), and the Singapore halal counts of 30 (Sukiya) and 17 (Yoshinoya) from the MUIS register.
- **Matsuya's halal status is genuinely unknown.** Say "hasn't announced theirs," never "no halal certification." One is a fact about disclosure; the other is a claim about the product.
