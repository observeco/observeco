10 Forces the Headlines Don't Show

Beneath the aggregate numbers, ten structural forces decide who survives. They rarely appear in any macroeconomic report.

Who is actually buying?
1

CPF suppresses consumption

20% employee + 17% employer locked away.58% gross savings rate. The high earner's S$20K/mo is not fully spendable.

2

The domestic helper is a regular expense

317,000 helpers in1 in5 households. Makes the dual-income family work. She is the one who goes to the shops.

3

The heartland-downtown geography

83% of new businesses register outside CBD. Premium demand concentrates downtown. Choose which geography you serve.

Who holds the power?
4

Platforms own the customer relationship

Shopee53%, top3 =99%. You pack the boxes. The platform knows your customer's name.

5

The SME finance ceiling

800 companies above S$100M revenue — barely moved since 2017. Banks lend against property, not ideas.

What are your actual options?
6

The Johor-Singapore SEZ

100 projects, 20,000 skilled jobs, special tax incentives. Front office in Singapore, back office in Johor.

7

The fiscal machine

Budget surpluses, sovereign reserves. A small business on the right side of policy rides the state's capacity.

8

Risk-aversion and high cost of failure

High rents, committed leases, sunk costs. Entrepreneurship skews toward side-hustles. Decisive competitors are rare.

The force changing all the others
9

AI is reshaping everything

14.5% SME adoption (tripled in a year). 213 new businesses a day. When execution is cheap for everyone, position decides who wins.

10

The state as competitor

Top3 state-linked companies control a fifth of market cap. DBS, Singtel, SIA — you can buy from the state, but you can also compete against it.

These forces are not abstractions in a government report. Each one shows up as a specific customer, a specific constraint, a specific opportunity. The business that names them can work with them. The business that ignores them gets worked over by them.
