The Ageing Is Three Separate Markets, Not One

One in five citizens is now 65+. It was one in eight ten years ago. It is headed to one in four. The failure to separate the three segments inside ageing is the single most expensive mistake a business can make.

The Three Markets Inside "Ageing"
The silver economy
Independence & care

The senior as consumer

Buying health, care, financial planning. They are going to live longer than any generation before them. The demand is independence, not medical.

The working elderly
67% still employed at 60–64

Both labour pool and customer

50% of those aged 65–69 still work. One of the highest rates in the world. They are at the same time a labour pool and a customer base.

The senior who lives alone
88,000 people

Buying everything

Meals, safety, transport, monitoring — because there is nobody at home to do it. The person she lets into her flat is the person she trusts.

A 65-year-old who is still working, and an 82-year-old living alone, are not the same customer. They are almost not the same species of customer. The ageing is real. The segmentation inside it is the opportunity. Treat "an ageing population" as one market and you will make the single most expensive error in this chapter.
