Small Island, Crowded Market — Book 1 · Part Four, Ch 1

F&B Is Not One Market — It Is Six

Each product category has different brands, different structures, and different words. A small business does not enter "F&B" — it enters one product category and takes one word.

Walled — two-name shelf

Delivery Platforms

Grab over half, foodpanda the main rival. Deliveroo exited. The shelf owns the customer, not the food.
Grab owns "delivery" — but it's a channel, not a brand
Walled — one giant

Quick-Service / Fast Food

McDonald's ~40% of the slice. KFC, Subway, Burger King, Domino's. Global chains with decades of brand.
McDonald's owns "fast" — the everyday meal word
Walled — landlords

Coffee Shops & Food Courts

Kimly, Koufu, Food Republic. The landlords of the stalls, not the food itself.
They own the venue, not the dish
Open — fragmented field

Full-Service Restaurants

Jumbo, Paradise, Putien, Dian Xiao Er. Domestic groups, no single name dominates.
The celebration meal word appears open
Open — contested, no leader

Coffee & Bubble Tea

Starbucks ~140, Luckin ~81, LiHo, KOI ~40, Gong Cha, Tiger Sugar. Contested, no clear leader.
The bubble-tea word: no clear owner, unverified
Fragmented — long tail

Hawker Stalls

Thousands of independents. No brands. The backbone of Singapore's food culture.
The hawker who owns a dish owns a word no rival can take

A small business does not enter "F&B"; it enters one product category, competes in one mental category, and takes one word. The delivery platform is a shelf, not a food brand. The coffee chain is contested, not owned. The hawker stall is the long tail where a specific dish can become a word. Choose your category, then own your word.
