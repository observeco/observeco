## Caption

> I spent 15 years in consulting and 18 in startups. And every week someone asks me
> the same thing: *how do I get customers to choose me?*
>
> Here's the part that took me years to see. Most owners can explain their product
> perfectly. Almost none can tell you why a customer picks them over the shop next
> door.
>
> Big companies have consultants. Small businesses have to figure it out themselves.
> And that's not fair.
>
> The businesses still finding their feet need clarity the most. Who are my real
> customers? Why should they choose me? What makes me different? Why aren't people
> buying? But traditional strategy consulting was never really built for them.
>
> That's where ObserveCo comes in.
>
> We observe your market, your customers and your real competitors. We uncover the
> patterns and gaps you can't see from the inside. We find the space you can
> credibly own. And we turn it into a clear reason for customers to choose you.
>
> Not more jargon. Not a 100-page strategy deck. Not advice based on assumptions.
> Just a clear understanding of what makes your business matter — and how to make
> customers see it too.
>
> Every brand you already trust did exactly this. Airbnb was "a cheap alternative
> to hotels" — $0.9B. Then "belong anywhere" — $4.8B. Avis was "second to Hertz"
> at ~10% share. Then "we're only No. 2, so we try harder" — ~29%. The product
> never changed. Only the reason to choose it did.
>
> Strategy shouldn't be a luxury for small businesses. ObserveCo exists to make
> strategic thinking accessible to the businesses that need it most.
>
> You may be small. But your strategy shouldn't be.
>
> If you're honest about where you're stuck, tell me your market in the comments. 👇
>

> #SingaporeEconomy #SmallBusinessSG #MarketingSingapore #BusinessGrowth

### Why slide 6 carries no pricing

The homepage justifies the fee against the cost of being wrong (S$8,000–138,000 /
7×–172×). That argument belongs **on the site**, where a reader is already
evaluating. On a cold IG scroll it reads as a sales pitch and breaks the
positioning arc Sean set: *problem → unfairness → who we are → what we promise*.
Slide 6 therefore closes on the promise ("the reason people would actually choose
you") and the three exclusions, and slide 8 lands the mission. Pricing is absent
from the whole carousel.

## Series note

This post sits **before** the Book-1 run (posts 01, 03–13). The Book-1 posts argue
from Singapore's structural data; this one answers the prior question a cold
audience has — *what is this business actually for?* Run it as the pinned
introduction, or as the first post of a new account.

Slide 7 deliberately uses a **ledger, not a bar chart**. Each brand has its own
before/after metric on its own scale, so proportional bars would encode a false
comparison (e.g. Avis's 10%→29% share against Wang Lao Ji's ¥100M→¥16B revenue).
The ledger states the word owned + the outcome and claims no shared axis.
