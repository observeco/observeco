## 2. Copyright: ideas are not protected, expression is

**The core principle, on which every jurisdiction agrees, including Singapore:** copyright protects the *expression* of an idea, not the idea itself.

- "Intellectual property law does not protect ideas or concepts, it protects the expression of those ideas." (UK IPO report on copyright & AI)
- "Ideas, facts, style and genre generally do not attract copyright protection." (NUS Law, on Singapore)
- Singapore's **Copyright Act 2021** (Cap. 21) is the governing statute, in force since 21 Nov 2021.

**What this means for our books:**
- ✅ **Safe to do:** Explain in our own words what positioning is, what "the battle for the mind" means, what the four marketing-warfare postures (defensive/offensive/flanking/guerrilla) are, and credit Ries, Trout, and Gu Junhui as the originators. This is what every marketing book in the world does; no one pays Ries a licence to explain his idea.
- ✅ **Safe to do:** Cite their books (*Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind*, *Marketing Warfare*) as sources.
- ⚠️ **NOT safe to do (and the scan confirms we don't):** Reproduce their enumerated lists ("The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing"), their specific "laws" verbatim, their chapter structures, their diagrams, or quote substantial passages. Closely paraphrasing an enumerated framework risks being found to reproduce expression, not just the idea.
- ⚠️ **NOT safe to do:** Presenting our *own* construction as *their* canonical framework (the "9 types of positioning" misattribution, which we already dropped).

**Facts are not protected by copyright at all.** Founding dates, store counts, revenue, market share, names, and historical events are facts. We can state them freely. What we cannot do is copy the *words* someone used to state them (see §6 on defamation, and §9 on sourcing).

**The scan result (both books):** 0 instances of "9 types," "22 immutable laws," "22 laws," or enumerated "law #" lists. 17 proper attributions to Ries/Trout/Gu Junhui. No blockquotes of their text. The long quoted strings are all our own prose in emphasis quotes. **Copyright risk: low.**

**Posture-framework de-enumeration (2026-08-31):** The four posture chapters previously presented "rules of good defence/offence/flanking" as numbered First/Second/Third/Fourth lists that tracked *Marketing Warfare*'s enumerated principles in sequence. These have been rewritten as flowing prose paragraphs (no numbered enumeration), removing the "reproduction of a distinctive structure" exposure. The guerrilla chapter's five moves and five traps are original content (speed/specialist/local champion/underserved corner/trusted referral) and remain enumerated. The appendix credits paragraph now states accurately that the book explains the postures in its own prose and order, and carries a non-affiliation line (the book is not affiliated with or endorsed by Ries, Trout, their estates, or Gu Junhui). The Gu Junhui framing was corrected from "one of the three men who built it" to "the man who carried it into Asia" (a translator/disciple, not a co-originator).

## 3. The one copyright pattern to keep sweeping for

The risk is not in what's there; it's in what could creep in during edits. Every time we mention a Ries/Trout/Gu concept, the sentence must be **our own paraphrase**, never a close restatement of a sentence from their books. The test: if a sentence could be read as "the source said X, almost in their words," rewrite it.

**Keep sweeping before publication for:** their enumerated lists, their named "laws," their diagrams, their chapter headings, and any passage that reads like a condensed quote. The current drafts are clean; this is a discipline for every future edit, not a present problem.

## 4. Trademark: naming real brands as case studies

**Nominative fair use protects us.** Using a competitor's (or any real company's) mark to refer to that company and its products — in commentary, comparison, or academic/editorial work — is a recognized fair use, in the US and in Singapore's framework.

- INTA: nominative fair use "generally applies to comparative advertising, parody and non-commercial use of trademarks in academic articles, media..."
- "Nominative fair use also protects the use of a competitor's mark in a comparative advertisement... such statements can qualify as nominative fair uses." (Finnegan, *Trademark Thoughts*)

**What this means:** Writing "Old Chang Kee owns the word 'curry puff'" or "Charles & Keith grew to nearly 700 stores" is commentary about real, identifiable companies — this is what nominative fair use is for. It is **not** using their logos, slogans, or brand marks as our own, and it is **not** implying endorsement or affiliation.

**The boundaries that keep us safe:**
- Use brand names **referentially** (to identify the subject of commentary), not decoratively or as our own branding.
- **Do not** reproduce their logos, wordmarks, slogans, or distinctive packaging.
- **Do not** imply they endorse us, sponsor us, or are affiliated with us.
- **Do not** use a brand name in our title, subtitle, or cover in a way that suggests the book is "official" or endorsed.

**The one thing to check:** our **book title** and cover. A title that used a famous brand's name could be a trademark issue. "From Small to Big" and "The Map" are generic and safe — but confirm no subtitle or cover element uses a registered mark.

## 5. Book title: copyright and trademark

- **Titles generally get no copyright protection.** Copyright does not protect short phrases, titles, or slogans as such. Two books with the same or similar titles is common and generally not copyright infringement.
- **Trademark protection only arises if the title is used "in the course of trade" as a badge of origin AND is confusingly similar to a registered mark.** "From Small to Big" and "The Map" are generic English phrases, highly unlikely to be registered marks in the book category. 
- **Check:** before finalising, run a quick search of the title on the Intellectual Property Office of Singapore (IPOS) trademark database and the US Patent & Trademark Office (USPTO) to confirm no book-category registration of the exact title. Low risk; this is a 10-minute diligence check, not a blocker.

## 6. Defamation: the one area that needs a fact pass (LOW-MODERATE)

**The law:** Defamation requires a **false statement of fact** (not opinion) that harms reputation. This is the common-law position and Singapore's Defamation Act 1957.

- "A person may be held liable only for false statements of fact—not for expressing an opinion, no matter how harsh." (common law; confirmed by multiple sources)
- "The two most fundamental defences... only a false statement of fact (as opposed to opinion) can be defamatory... proving the truth of an allegedly defamatory statement is always a valid defence." (Wikipedia, *Defamation*; reflects common law)
- Singapore applies common-law defamation principles and the Defamation Act 1957 (Chambers Global Practice Guide).

**What this means for our books:**
- ✅ We make **positive, admiring** statements about the brands (they're case studies in success). Positive commentary does not harm reputation and cannot be defamatory.
- ✅ We state **facts** (founding dates, founders, store counts) — these are verifiable and, where sourced, provably true (the truth defence).
- ✅ We state **opinions/analysis** ("X owns the word 'curry puff'") — analysis and opinion are not actionable as defamation.
- ⚠️ **The residual risk:** if any *specific factual* claim about a brand or person were **false**, and harmful, it could ground a claim. This is why **source verification matters for legal reasons, not just accuracy**. Every store count, revenue figure, founder name, and founding date must be verified (see the source-verification report).

**The specific brands we name as companies** (Charles & Keith, Grab, BreadTalk, Secretlab, OSIM, etc.) are **companies**, not individuals. Companies can sue for defamation in Singapore, but they must show the statement caused (or is likely to cause) serious financial loss. Our positive, admiring commentary poses negligible defamation risk — the risk would only arise from a **false negative factual claim**, which we don't make.

**Founders and individuals:** We name founders (Ian Ang, Charles & Keith Wong, George Quek, Ho Kwon Ping, etc.) and Lee Kuan Yew. All our statements about them are either true facts or positive/admiring analysis. No disparagement. Risk is low, **provided** the facts attributed to each named person are verified.

## 7. Image rights / right of publicity — the LKY chapter (LOW IN SINGAPORE)

This is the finding that most de-risks the LKY chapter and the living-founder stories:

**Singapore does NOT recognise a standalone right of publicity or image right.**
- "Image rights, and in particular, the right to stop others from unauthorized use of one's own image, is not presently recognized as a standalone concept in Singapore." (Marcus Liu, Amica Law, *Asia IP*, 2023)
- "In Singapore, both the rights of publicity and privacy are neither recognised under the common law nor statute." (David Tan, NUS Law, SSRN)
- Singapore protects image/persona only indirectly, through: the Copyright Act, the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), the Prevention of Harassment Act, the law of confidence, the penal code (obscene images), and the tort of **passing off** (false endorsement).

**What this means:**
- Writing that Lee Kuan Yew "positioned Singapore against Malacca, Penang, and a hundred countries" is historical commentary about a deceased public figure — not an endorsement, not commercial misappropriation of his image. Low risk in Singapore.
- Naming living founders in a factual, admiring case-study context is commentary, not false endorsement. The **passing-off** route (false endorsement) requires us to imply a founder endorses us — we do not.
- ⚠️ **Watch the distribution channel:** If the book is sold in the **US or India**, those jurisdictions have statutory/recognised rights of publicity with different (sometimes post-mortem, sometimes very strong) protections. **Book 1 and Book 2 should not use any individual's photo, likeness, or distinctive image on the cover or in marketing.** Text-only biographical commentary is much safer than image use.

**Rule:** Keep the LKY and founder material **text-only** (words about them, not images of them). Never use a person's photo or likeness in the book or its marketing without permission. If any image of a person or brand appears later, clear it.

Everything in both books is built from **published** statistics (SingStat, MOM, MTI), **published** brand histories (company pages, Wikipedia, established media), and **our own analysis**. We use no confidential information, no non-public figures, and no one's internal data. Risk: negligible.

## 9. The sourcing discipline that protects us (copyright + defamation)

Because we state many specific facts, the discipline that protects us is:
1. **Every specific fact** (founding date, founder name, store count, revenue, market share, macro figure) **must trace to a source** — currently the manuscripts carry "Sources and confidence" blocks per chapter. Keep these.
2. **State facts from sources; express analysis as ours.** Never copy a source's sentence structure closely when stating a fact.
3. **Flag confidence.** Where a figure is directional (e.g., market share), label it as such. The manuscripts already do this ("Moderate-High," "directional and confidence-labeled").
4. **Fix any figure that fails verification** before publication — a wrong store count is not just an accuracy problem; it's the one thing that could ground a defamation or misrepresentation claim if negative (ours are positive, so the practical risk is accuracy/reputation, not litigation).
